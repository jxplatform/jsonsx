---
title: The R&D Credit Is Not Just for Laboratories
excerpt: Software teams, manufacturers, and product companies routinely qualify for research credits they never claim. Here is what actually counts.
date: September 8, 2025
author: Marcus Bell
order: 5
---

The research and development tax credit carries a reputation for belonging to pharmaceutical companies and hardware labs. In practice the definition is far broader, and many ordinary businesses leave the credit unclaimed simply because they assume it does not apply to them.

The test is about the nature of the work, not the industry. If your team is developing or improving a product, process, or piece of software, resolving genuine technical uncertainty through experimentation, that activity often qualifies. Building new features, automating a production line, or engineering a more reliable system can all count.

Qualified costs usually include the wages of the people doing and directly supervising the work, the supplies consumed in testing, and a portion of what you pay outside contractors. For a company with even a modest engineering payroll, the credit can reach into six figures and, unlike a deduction, it reduces tax dollar for dollar.

The catch is documentation. The credit rewards businesses that can tie specific projects to specific costs and show the technical uncertainty they were working through. Capturing that trail as the work happens, rather than reconstructing it under deadline, is the difference between a credit you can defend and one you quietly forfeit.
