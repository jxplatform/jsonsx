Most of the tax bill you will pay next April is decided in the final quarter of the year, not in the filing season that follows. By the time the return is prepared, the levers that actually move the number have already been set. The best-run companies treat the fourth quarter as a planning window rather than a reporting deadline.

Start with timing. If your business uses cash-basis accounting, you can often defer income into January or accelerate deductible expenses into December, smoothing your taxable income across two years. Prepaying vendors, topping up retirement contributions, and placing equipment in service before year end all shift deductions into the current period.

Then look at elections you may be leaving on the table. Bonus depreciation, the Section 179 expensing limit, and the qualified business income deduction each have thresholds that reward a little planning. A conversation in November is worth far more than a scramble in March.

Finally, document everything while it is fresh. Reasonable compensation, home-office use, and vehicle mileage are all defensible when the records are contemporaneous and vague when they are reconstructed later. A clean file is the cheapest insurance you can buy against an audit.
