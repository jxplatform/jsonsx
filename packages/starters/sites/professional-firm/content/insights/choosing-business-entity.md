The entity you choose is one of the few decisions that touches taxes, liability, fundraising, and administration all at once. It is worth getting right early, because changing structure later can trigger tax and legal friction you would rather avoid.

A limited liability company is the flexible default for most small businesses. It separates your personal assets from the company, keeps compliance light, and by default passes profits straight through to your personal return. For many owner-operators, that simplicity is exactly what they need.

Electing to be taxed as an S-corporation can reduce self-employment tax once profits are consistent, because only your reasonable salary is subject to payroll tax while the remaining distribution is not. The trade-off is more formality: a real payroll, a separate return, and stricter rules about who can own shares.

A C-corporation makes sense when you plan to raise venture capital or reinvest profits for growth rather than distribute them. It can issue multiple classes of stock and qualify for certain investor tax benefits, at the cost of a second layer of tax on dividends. The right answer depends on where you are heading, not just where you are today, which is exactly why the choice deserves a conversation rather than a default.
