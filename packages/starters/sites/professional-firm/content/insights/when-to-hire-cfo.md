---
title: When Does a Growing Company Need a CFO?
excerpt: You may not need a full-time finance chief yet, but you probably need the function. A look at the signals and the fractional middle ground.
date: September 30, 2025
author: Dana Whitfield
order: 4
---

Founders often ask whether it is time to hire a chief financial officer. The more useful question is whether the company has outgrown the person currently doing the finance work in the margins of their real job. When strategic financial decisions start landing on someone who was never meant to own them, the function has arrived even if the title has not.

There are a few reliable signals. Board meetings now require forward-looking analysis rather than a look in the rear-view mirror. You are raising capital, servicing debt covenants, or fielding diligence requests. Pricing, hiring, and expansion decisions all hinge on numbers that take days to assemble and still feel uncertain. Each of these is a sign that finance has become strategic.

For most companies between one and twenty million in revenue, a full-time executive is more capacity than the business can use or afford. This is where a fractional CFO fits: senior judgment applied a few days a month, focused on forecasting, fundraising, and the decisions that actually move enterprise value.

The goal is not to add overhead. It is to put an experienced hand on the financial wheel before a costly mistake makes the case for you. The companies that bring in that judgment early tend to raise on better terms, avoid expensive missteps, and reach the point where a full-time hire is genuinely justified.
