---
title: Why Profitable Companies Still Run Out of Cash
excerpt: Profit and cash are not the same thing. A thirteen-week forecast is the single most useful tool for a business that is growing faster than its bank balance.
date: November 12, 2025
author: Marcus Bell
category: Cash Flow
order: 2
---

A business can be profitable on paper and still miss payroll. Profit is an accounting measure that recognizes revenue when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred. Cash is the money actually in the account on a given morning. Growth widens the gap between the two, because you pay for inventory, wages, and marketing long before your customers pay you.

The remedy is not complicated, but it is often neglected. A rolling thirteen-week cash forecast lays out expected receipts and disbursements week by week, so you can see a shortfall three weeks before it arrives rather than three days after. Thirteen weeks is long enough to give you room to act and short enough to forecast with real confidence.

Build the forecast from the bottom up. List the invoices you expect to collect and the bills you know are coming, then layer in payroll, rent, taxes, and loan payments. Update it every week against what actually happened, and your estimates will sharpen quickly.

Once the forecast is in place, it changes how you make decisions. You can time a large purchase for a strong collection week, negotiate supplier terms with evidence in hand, and approach your lender before a crunch rather than during one. Visibility turns cash from a source of anxiety into a lever you control.
