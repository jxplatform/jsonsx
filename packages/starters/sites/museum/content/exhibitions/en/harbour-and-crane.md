The city was a mudflat with a jetty until the first stone quay went in, and everything the museum owns from the four centuries since is, in one way or another, about the water. This exhibition puts 214 of those objects in one room and lets them argue with each other.

A shipwright's adze sits beside the harbour-master's 1782 ledger, open at the page where he recorded the same vessel arriving twice in one week and struck the second entry out. The salvaged jib of the Quai 4 crane — dismantled in 1962, kept in pieces in a shed for fifty years, and reassembled here for the first time — runs the length of the room at head height.

What the objects have in common is that none of them was made to be looked at. They were made to be used, and they carry the wear of it. The labels say who used them, where that is possible, and say so plainly when it is not.
