Potters worked the clay bank upriver, salters worked the flats, and the thread came off looms in rooms above both. Three trades, one economy, and a set of objects that were never displayed together until now.

Sixty pieces are shown at the height they were made at — the pots low, the salt moulds at bench height, the woven work hung so you can see both faces. Where a workshop survives in the city, its address is on the label; four of them are still working, and two take visitors on Saturday mornings.

The courtyard holds the largest object in the museum's care: a salt pan, six metres across, moved here in sections in 2019 and reassembled on the gravel where the rain can reach it, which is what it was built for.
