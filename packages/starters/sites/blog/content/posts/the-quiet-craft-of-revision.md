There is a myth that good writers get it right the first time. They do not. What they have is a higher tolerance for looking at their own bad sentences, over and over, until the bad ones are gone.

A first draft is a machine for finding out what you actually think. It is allowed to be clumsy. Its only job is to exist. Everything after that — the trimming, the reordering, the merciless cutting of the clever line you were most proud of — is where the writing actually happens.

## Cutting is a form of respect

The hardest edits are the ones that remove something good. Not something bad; a bad line is easy to delete. A good line that does not belong is the real test. Keeping it is a kindness to yourself and a small cruelty to the reader, who now has to carry a sentence that leads nowhere.

> Write drunk, edit sober, the saying goes. I would put it differently: write generously, edit as if every word costs you something.

The trick is to read your own work as a stranger would — someone who did not sit through the three hours it took to produce the paragraph, who owes it no loyalty, who will simply stop reading if it stops earning their attention.

## Revision is thinking, continued

We treat revision as cleanup, as if the ideas were already finished and we are just wiping down the counters. But most of what I believe about a subject, I discover on the fourth read, when the argument I thought I was making turns out to be a smaller, truer argument hiding underneath.

So I have stopped rushing to publish. A piece that has only been drafted is a piece that has only been guessed at. Give it a week. Come back a stranger. Cut what you can no longer defend, and keep only what earns its place.
