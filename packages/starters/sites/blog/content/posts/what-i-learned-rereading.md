I keep the books I love, and every few years I read one of them again. Not to discover the plot — I know the plot — but to find out what has changed. Because the book has not changed. The words are exactly where I left them. The only variable in the experiment is me.

This is the quiet gift of rereading. A first read is about the book. A second read, years later, is about the distance between who you were and who you are. The pages become a fixed point against which you can finally measure your own drift.

## The margins remember

In my oldest books there are notes in handwriting I barely recognize as mine. A younger person underlined that passage, wrote _yes_ in the margin beside an idea I now find naive, and skipped entirely the paragraph that today seems like the whole point.

> We do not read the same book twice, because we are not the same reader twice.

It is humbling and a little vertiginous to meet that earlier reader. They were so sure. They missed so much. And of course, the reader I am now is missing things too — passages I skim past that some future self will underline and wonder how I ever overlooked.

## Reading as a long conversation

So I have come to think of my shelf less as a collection and more as a set of appointments with my future self. Each book I reread is a conversation held across years, with someone who is me and also not me, who understood things I have forgotten and misunderstood things I have since learned.

Buy fewer books, maybe. But keep the ones that matter, and go back to them. The best of them will have grown while you were away — which is to say, you will have.
